# Damascus transit data survives only in fragments

As of October 2026 there is no official map, line list, timetable, GTFS feed or open dataset for Damascus or Rif Dimashq public transport; what exists is a reconstructable but fragmentary picture assembled from governorate decisions, wire-service reports and a very thin OpenStreetMap layer. The strongest usable assets are (1) an inventory of roughly **35 named city servis lines** embedded in Damascus Governorate fuel and GPS decisions of 2022–2023, (2) a Rif Dimashq line catalogue of about **25 town-to-garage pairs** anchored on four "exchange centres" that the governorate has been enforcing since 2024, (3) **WGS84 coordinates for about 20 garages and 45 landmarks** pulled live from OpenStreetMap, and (4) a dated fare chain ending in the **September 2026 committee tariff of 40 new SYP per bus ride and 40–70 new SYP per servis line**, with field evidence that riders actually pay 5–15 new SYP more. What does not exist anywhere public is ordered stop sequences (only two lines on Wikiroutes, dated 2021, plus 13 OSM route relations), route geometry for the microbus network, any headway or operating-hours data, the full text of Ministry of Transport Decision 108 (the per-line fare table), or coordinates for several key garages (Wazzan, Sayyida Aisha, Nahr Aisha, the new Street 30 centre). The app can therefore be seeded today with real garages, real line names, real termini and a defensible fare model, but routes between termini must be digitised from waypoints and then verified in the field, and fares must be stored with date, currency and official-versus-charged status because the tariff has changed five times in twenty months. The recommended fill strategy is a three-layer pipeline: desk digitisation from the sources below, direct acquisition from the General Establishment for Passenger Transport and the governorate's Telegram channel, and crowdsourced GPS traces contributed back to OSM under ODbL.

## Thirty-five city servis lines are known by name, none by geometry

The single most important finding is that Damascus servis lines have **no official numbering and no published map**; every source from a 2013 essay to a 2026 governorate decision identifies lines only by the Arabic origin–destination name painted on the roof sign ([Longreads](https://longreads.com/2014/01/20/the-end-of-the-line-a-microbus-map-of-damascus/)). The fullest "official" enumerations are incidental: a Damascus Governorate statement of 3 November 2022 on diesel supply named **30 lines**, including مزة 86 خزان برامكة, مشروع دمر جسر السيد الرئيس, دويلعة كراجات, مهاجرين صناعة, ركن الدين الشيخ خالد جسر السيد الرئيس, مساكن برزة شارع الثورة and سومرية برامكة كراجات ([SANA, 3 Nov 2022](https://archive.sana.sy/%D8%AA%D8%B2%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%82%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84)). Three GPS "start/end of line" enforcement notices then added lines that the first list missed: 14 lines in May 2023 including جرمانا كراج الست, جرمانا باب توما and مزة جبل كراجات ([Al-Watan via Nabd, 23 May 2023](https://nabd.com/s/119971407-18c9f3/)), 17 lines in June 2023 adding القابون, التضامن مسبق الصنع الفحامة and the Kiswa and Sayyida Zainab suburban lines ([SANA, 6 Jun 2023](https://archive.sana.sy/%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%B9%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%B2%D8%A9-%D8%A8%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B7-%D9%88%D9%86%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%AA%D9%87-%D9%84%D9%80-17-%D8%AE%D8%B7-%D8%B3%D9%8A)), and 10 lines in October 2023 adding الدوار الشمالي, جوبر عباسيين ابن النفيس كلية الهمك and the full form مخيم فلسطين مخيم اليرموك كراجات برامكة ([Enab Baladi, 3 Oct 2023](https://www.enabbaladi.net/663947/)). The union of these four lists is the best available proxy for the licensed city network, because they enumerate exactly the lines that refuelled in Damascus and were GPS-tracked; they should be treated as a 2022–2023 baseline, not the current state, since both the governorate and the Ministry's General Establishment for Passenger Transport have said repeatedly in 2025–2026 that they are "re-studying" lines ([Al-Modon, 31 Jan 2026](https://www.almodon.com/economy/2026/01/31/%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82-%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9)).

Everything else is endpoint names only.

The public bus network is even thinner on paper. The General Establishment for Passenger Transport says Damascus has "about **43 internal transport lines**" ([Syria TV, 13 Mar 2026](https://www.syria.tv/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%83%D9%87%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%86%D9%82%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%8A-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82)), but that figure is almost certainly licensed concessions rather than running services, because the public company's Damascus fleet in June 2025 was **1,304 buses on paper with only 75 operating**, 35 of them post-2020, plus 22 Belarus-donated MAZ buses ([SANA, 4 Jun 2025](https://sana.sy/locals/2228387/)). Only six bus lines are named in official 2025–2026 sources: the three trunk lines tendered for 150 electric buses (باب توما – جسر الحرية with 30, مهاجرين – صناعة with 70, سومرية – كراجات with 50, on 10-year private concessions announced 4 March 2026) ([SANA, 13 Mar 2026](https://sana.sy/locals/2425353/)) and three reactivated in June 2025 at a 1,000 old SYP fare: اليرموك – الحميدية, مساكن برزة – شارع الثورة – ساحة العباسيين and حاميش – مساكن برزة – برامكة – كلية الآداب – المزة ([Enab Baladi, 8 Jun 2025](https://www.enabbaladi.net/758398/)). No tender award or electric bus in service had been reported by 13 October 2026, and the fact that servis were still charging on مهاجرين – صناعة in September 2026 shows the "clearing" of those corridors has not been enforced. The one genuinely new scheduled service is the Baramkeh garages ↔ Damascus International Airport bus from 11 October 2026 with **14 departures a day** ([SANA, 10 Oct 2026](https://sana.sy/locals/2602722/)), which OSM already carries as relations 4111623/4111655.

## Rif Dimashq lines funnel into four exchange centres with known coordinates

For the suburban network, the governing fact is policy rather than geography: since August 2024 Damascus Governorate has been pushing an estimated 25,000 Rif vehicles out of the centre into four "exchange centres" (المراكز التبادلية) named as كراج العباسيين, السومرية, تحت جسر الوزان and كراج شارع 30, with a new "South departure centre" replacing كراج باب مصلى ([Al-Watan, 1 Aug 2024](https://alwatan.sy/سرافيس-ممنوع-وصولها-إلى-شارع-الثورة-وم/); [Athr Press, 1 Sep 2024](https://athrpress.com/من-بينها-نقل-كراج-باب-مصلى-محافظة-دمشق/محليات/)). The rollout has been staged and uneven: Eastern Ghouta and Qalamoun servis were anchored at Abbasiyeen by September 2024; on 16 May 2025 the traffic administration moved nine Wadi Barada and Dimas-cluster lines (جديدة الشيباني، عين الفيجة، أشرفية الوادي، جديدة الوادي، قرى الشام، عين الخضرة، بسيمة، ضاحية الفردوس، مساكن الديماس) from جسر الحرية to the Wazzan lot and الصبورة plus ديماس البلد to Sumariyah, backed by a 130,000 SYP fine and three-day impound for leaving one's assigned route ([Syria TV, 17 May 2025](https://www.syria.tv/أزمة-تنتقل-بلا-حل-وكلفة-تتصاعد-من-يدفع-ثمن-قرار-نقل-مواقف-السرافيس-خارج-دمشق؟); [Enab Baladi, 1 Jun 2025](https://www.enabbaladi.net/757082/)); the gradual move of كراج السيدة زينب, holding **more than 1,000 microbuses**, to the Street 30 centre began 29 March 2026 ([Syrian Days, 28 Mar 2026](https://syriandays.com/index.php?id=79449&page=show_det)); and the Daraa/Suwayda intercity garage moved from باب مصلى to Street 30 in Yarmouk on 1 July 2026 ([Enab Baladi, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.enabbaladi.net/813784/)). For the app this means a Rif-to-centre journey is now structurally **two or three legs** (microbus to the exchange centre, then a city microbus or bus), and sources consistently report doubled journey times after each relocation, with riders from Ashrafiyat al-Wadi who used to reach ساحة الأمويين in under 30 minutes now taking three vehicles ([Enab Baladi, 1 Jun 2025](https://www.enabbaladi.net/757082/)).

Wikidata's own town coordinates are rounded to a sixtieth of a degree and should not be used in preference to the OSM place node.

## Fares changed five times in twenty months and now sit at 40–70 new SYP

The fare history is the best-documented part of the whole system, but only if every figure is tagged with date and currency, because Syria removed two zeros from the pound at the turn of 2025/2026 (**100 old SYP = 1 new SYP**), with central-bank exchange from 3 January 2026, a 90-day extendable dual-circulation period and new notes of 5–500 ([Al Jazeera, 3 Jan 2026](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2026/1/3/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%A7-%D8%A3%D8%B5%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%AB%D9%82%D8%A9)); sources through May 2026 still quoted old-pound amounts, and by August 2026 old notes had lost legal tender status while 5- and 10-pound coins remained scarce enough that drivers refuse to make change on a 35-SYP fare ([Oil News Agency / Independent Arabia, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.oilnewsagency.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A9-%D9%83%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B3%D9%88/)).

The September 2026 tariff is the figure the app should carry as "official", with the caveat that Economist-sy stressed the tables had **not been formally adopted or published as binding** at the time of writing and exist only as images in the article, while Kassioun ten days later treated the 40 / 40–70 figures as in force. What riders actually paid in mid-September is better documented than the tariff itself: مهاجرين–صناعة, دويلعة–برامكة and كفرسوسة–برامكة went from 30 to 40–50 new SYP, مزة جبل–كراجات from 35 to 45–50, فلسطين–الكراجات from 40 to 50–60, الدوار الجنوبي from 35 to 40–50 and ميدان–برزة from 45 to 55–65 ([Enab Baladi, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.enabbaladi.net/824978/)). The pattern that holds across every year is a "charged" premium of roughly **+5 to +15 new SYP over tariff**, plus rounding up to the nearest 5 or 10 because small coins are scarce, and doubling after about 6 pm, a practice documented as far back as pre-2020 and again in March 2024 on Jarmana–Bab Touma ([Halab Today TV, 1 Mar 2024](https://halabtodaytv.net/archives/268905)). Enforcement rests on windscreen stickers printed by the traffic-engineering directorate, surprise inspections by the General Establishment, and the Damascus Governorate "Mahloula" (محلولة) complaints app, whose QR codes were to be printed on the new stickers from May 2026 ([SANA, 10 May 2026](https://sana.sy/governorates/damascus/2472323/)); there is no ticketing, no smart card and no transfer discount, so every boarding is a separate cash fare.

For Rif Dimashq the structure is different and less documented. No published km-band table exists for 2024–2026; fares are per-line outputs of a cost model (fuel, oil, maintenance, depreciation, route nature, profit margin) run by governorate and GEPT committees and, in some municipalities, by local councils, which in February 2025 set Jdeidet Artouz → Baramkeh at 4,500, Jdeidet Artouz → Sumariyah at 2,500, Artouz → Damascus at 5,000 and Artouz → Qatana at 2,000 old SYP ([Al-Watan, 8 Feb 2025](https://alwatan.sy/تعرفة-جديدة-للنقل-في-جديدة-عرطوز-البلد/)). By January 2026 Rif fares ran "from 4,000 up to 18,000 old SYP" for Zabadani and Sarghaya ([Al-Modon, 31 Jan 2026](https://www.almodon.com/economy/2026/01/31/%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82-%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9)), the Damascus–Tal line was cut by decision from 5,000 to 3,500 old SYP in February 2026 against driver protest ([Al-Watan, 27 Feb 2026](https://alwatan.sy/سرافيس-زعلانة-في-ريف-دمشق-اعتراضاً-عل/)), and after the September 2026 fuel rise Rif lines reached **up to 180 new SYP** with the Quneitra line Khan Arnabeh → Sumariyah officially 235 new SYP but paid at 240–250 ([Kassioun](https://kassioun.org/syria/item/86774:t-rft-almwaslat-thrq-jywb-alswryyn); [Al-Watan, 15 Sep 2026](https://alwatan.sy/%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B9-%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%82%D9%84-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%B1%D8%A9-30-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A6%D8%A9/)). Reading these scattered points together, Rif fares in late 2026 scale from roughly 40 new SYP for inner-ring towns to about 180 for 40–50 km lines, i.e. on the order of 3–4 new SYP per kilometre above a 35–40 SYP floor; this is an inference from the data, not a published rule, and it should be displayed in the app as an estimate until the Rif Dimashq transport directorate's September 2026 tariff, which was issued but never published in any accessible source, is obtained.

## Service runs fill-and-go with no timetables and shrinking supply

No source in 2024–2026 states first or last departure times, headways or average speeds for any line; servis leave garages when full, and the only quantified operating data are rider and driver statements made in complaints. The Wikiroutes 2021 entries give 10- and 15-minute headways from 07:00 to 22:50 on the two Muhajireen lines, and those remain the only frequency figures for the city network ([Wikiroutes 76515](https://wikiroutes.info/en/damascus?routes=76515)). On the Rif side the relocation of departure points has collapsed productivity: the Jdeidat al-Shaybani line fell from **6–8 trips a day to 2** when moved to Wazzan, even though its route shortened from 20 km to 13 km, and Dahiyat al-Firdous riders who "used to fill a servis in minutes under Jisr al-Hurriya" now wait two hours ([Syria TV, 17 May 2025](https://www.syria.tv/أزمة-تنتقل-بلا-حل-وكلفة-تتصاعد-من-يدفع-ثمن-قرار-نقل-مواقف-السرافيس-خارج-دمشق؟); [SY24, 16 May 2025](https://www.sy-24.com/149581/)); Jaramana drivers manage only about four 23-km round trips a day because of congestion ([North Press, 23 Dec 2025](https://npasyria.com/229398/)); and at كراج نهر عيشة the Sbeineh–Hujaira servis load "one or two passengers after more than six hours" ([Enab Baladi, 28 Sep 2025](https://www.enabbaladi.net/775716/)). Fleet figures conflict but agree on scale: GEPT counts **~5,200 servis in Damascus and ~8,500 in the Rif** ([Al-Modon](https://www.almodon.com/economy/2026/01/31/%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82-%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9)), while the land-transport union puts it at 15,400 with 3,400 on city lines and 12,000 on Rif lines ([Al-Watan, 24 Feb 2026](https://alwatan.sy/15400-%D8%B3%D8%B1%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82-%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A8%D8%AA/)). Strikes are a recurring disruption the app cannot predict but should be able to flag: partial stoppages hit مهاجرين–صناعة and the Mezzeh lines on 24 November 2025 and the three Jaramana lines for three days from 21 December 2025, each time after a fare cut ([Syria TV, 21 Dec 2025](https://www.syria.tv/%D8%A5%D8%B6%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A6%D9%8A-%D9%84%D9%80%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%B4%D9%82-%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%8B-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%AE%D9%81%D8%B6-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9)). The practical modelling consequence is that waiting time, not in-vehicle time, dominates door-to-door duration at peak, that peaks can only be inferred as roughly 07:00–09:00 and 16:00–18:00 from "every morning" and "every evening" complaints, and that reliability after 18:00 and on Fridays is materially lower.

## Open geodata is thin: 13 OSM relations, no GTFS, ODbL terms

OpenStreetMap is the only machine-readable source and it covers the road network and garages well but the transit network barely at all. An Overpass query over the city bounding box (33.40, 36.15, 33.62, 36.45) on 13 October 2026 returned **12 `route=bus` and 1 `route=minibus` relations, zero `route_master` and zero `route=share_taxi`**, and 135 stop or station features of which only 36 carry a name ([Overpass API](https://lz4.overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The relations with geometry are مزة جبل – كراجات (4108101, reverse 4108338, 62 ways but a single platform node), مستشفى – باب توما (4110300), Souq al-Hamidiyya – Midan (4110558/4110592), the airport bus pair (4111623/4111655), Somaria – Thaura (4113584/4113624), سيرفيس مساكن برزة مسبق الصنع – شارع الثورة (14469961, reverse 14995426 tagged minibus) and برامكة جديدة عرطوز from البرامكة (سانا) (20148903), plus one untagged relation 20148905; the contiguous 2014-era ids show a single mapper's effort, with only three sporadic additions since ([OSM relation 4108101](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/4108101)). That is at most seven distinct lines against a network of several thousand vehicles, so OSM covers well under five percent of the microbus network. Several `amenity=bus_station` nodes around 33.49, 36.31 tagged `trolleybus=yes` with `operator=Charging Point Nr.1..3` are a student project and must be excluded. Practical fetching notes matter for a production pipeline: overpass-api.de returns HTTP 406 without a custom User-Agent and the main endpoint was repeatedly "too busy", while `lz4.overpass-api.de` answered on the first or second try, so the app should host its own Overpass instance or build from a Geofabrik Syria PBF extract rather than hit public mirrors.

No GTFS feed exists. The MobilityData catalogue CSV of 3,532 feeds has no Syrian row, Transitland's public listing shows no Syrian operator, and the OSM list of GTFS feeds has no Syrian entry ([MobilityData catalog](https://storage.googleapis.com/storage/v1/b/mdb-csv/o/sources.csv?alt=media); [Transitland](https://www.transit.land/feeds?search=syria); [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/List_of_GTFS_feeds)). Moovit coverage could not be confirmed because its site returns an empty bot-challenge response, and the only app claiming Damascus route data is "Damascus Guide" (iOS, released 10 July 2026), which names no data source or licence and disclaims government affiliation ([mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/damascus-guide/6761196550)). Academic and NGO holdings add nothing georeferenced for the present day: the JICA 1999 Damascus urban transport study exists as nine scanned PDF volumes and is the only document likely to contain a drawn whole-city bus network, but it predates the war-era changes by a quarter century ([JICA Open Report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/710/710/710_313_11511896.html)); HDX offers only WFP's OSM road extract and OCHA boundaries ([HDX](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/syr_roads_streets)); and the governorate's own GPS programme, which installed about 9,000 trackers on public-transport vehicles in January 2023 and tracks 8,400 servis, has never published a line inventory ([Enab Baladi](https://www.enabbaladi.net/621727/)). Licensing is simple but has one consequence: OSM and the HDX extract are ODbL, requiring "© OpenStreetMap contributors" attribution and, if the app's stop and garage table is publicly used, release of that derived database under ODbL, while routing results shown to users are produced works needing attribution only; Wikidata is CC0 and OCHA boundaries are reported as CC BY 4.0 ([OSM wiki Syria](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Syria)). Because any route geometry the project collects would be contributed back to OSM anyway, the share-alike obligation is an asset rather than a constraint.

## Fill the gaps with a three-layer pipeline, not a single source

The evidence supports a clear data architecture. Lines should be modelled as **named origin–destination pairs with an optional intermediate token**, exactly as the roof signs and fare stickers encode them, because "مشروع دمر – مساكن الحرس – جسر الرئيس" and "مشروع دمر – جسر الرئيس" are two distinct routings and "كراجات" alone means Abbasiyeen/Pullman while "كراج الست" means Sayyida Zainab. Stations should be seeded from the OSM garage coordinates above plus the 36 named "موقف" nodes, with neighbourhood centroids as destination placeholders where no stop exists, each flagged as "garage", "stop" or "placeholder". Fares need a schema of official amount, charged range, currency (new SYP), effective date, issuing authority and line-length band, since the September 2026 bands (40/45/50/55/70) and the observed +5 to +15 premium are both real and both needed; the app should display the charged range and round to multiples of 5. Operating status needs a per-line flag for "scheduled to be cleared for electric buses", "stop relocated" and "strike reported", because the three trunk corridors and every Rif exchange-centre line have changed terminus at least once since 2024 and announced dates slip (the Karaj al-Sitt move was promised "within a month" in September 2024 and began in March 2026) ([Athr Press, 14 Sep 2024](https://athrpress.com/مصدر-بمحافظة-دمشق-نقل-كراج-السيدة-زينب/محليات/)).

The first layer is desk digitisation and can be completed now. It consists of loading the roughly 35 city lines and 25 Rif lines from the sources above, extracting the 13 OSM relations' way geometry with the Overpass query `relation(id:...); out body; >; out skel qt;`, snapping the two Wikiroutes stop lists and the al-Areen, Zahira–Ush al-Warwar and Mouwasat–Bayrouni waypoint sequences to the OSM road network, and generating provisional geometry for the remaining lines by shortest-path routing between terminus and named intermediate points on the OSM road graph, clearly marked as unverified. The second layer is official acquisition, which is where the largest single gains lie: the full text of Ministry of Transport Decision 108 of 17 March 2025 (Enab Baladi says it obtained a copy) would deliver a per-line, per-kilometre fare table for both governorates; the Damascus fare committee's September 2026 sticker tables exist as printed documents at the traffic-engineering directorate; the Rif Dimashq transport directorate issued its own September 2026 tariff; and the governorate's GPS tracker data, if shared, would resolve geometry for thousands of vehicles at once. Both the Ministry of Transport and the governorate publish line and fare decisions primarily on Telegram, so the channel مديرية إعلام دمشق should be ingested continuously as the authoritative change feed ([SANA, 13 Mar 2026](https://sana.sy/locals/2425353/); [Telegram](https://t.me/s/damascusgovrnorat)). The third layer is field and crowd collection: riding each line end to end with a smartphone GPS logger produces stop sequences and geometry in a day per hub, in-app trip logging by early users refines headways and actual fares by time of day, and both can be contributed to OSM as `route=minibus` relations with `from`, `to` and Arabic `name` tags, which simultaneously satisfies ODbL and makes the project the de facto maintainer of Damascus transit in OSM. Field work should prioritise the four garages missing coordinates entirely (Wazzan, Sayyida Aisha, Nahr Aisha, the Street 30 centre), the three electric-bus corridors whose status changes first, and the Abbasiyeen and Sumariyah bays, where which line loads from which bay is unknown.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the problem: the obstacle is not that Damascus transit data is secret but that it has never been assembled, because the system has run on roof signs, windscreen stickers and fill-and-go departures since before the war, and the institutions that regulate it (a 2025-created ministry establishment, a governorate committee and a traffic branch) coordinate through Telegram posts rather than datasets. That makes the first mover who digitises the network the owner of the only reference, which is a stronger position than licensing a feed would have been, but it also means the app's data will decay fast: five tariff changes and at least six terminal relocations in twenty months, with a "temporary" September 2026 fuel rise likely to trigger a downward fare revision within weeks of any fuel cut, as the November 2025 precedent showed.

The practical implication is to treat the dataset as a living, versioned artefact rather than a one-time import: ship with real garages, real line names and the September 2026 fare bands, show geometry confidence and fare "official versus charged" explicitly in the interface, and invest the project's effort in the change feed and the OSM contribution loop. The one acquisition that would change the picture overnight is a copy of Decision 108 and the governorate's GPS line inventory; everything else can be built incrementally from the fragments documented here.
